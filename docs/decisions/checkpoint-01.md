# Checkpoint 1 decisions: Swipe a level

One line per decision: what was decided, and the citation from the frozen plan, copied rather than paraphrased.

D1. The vocabulary is the HSK 3.0 syllabus word list from github.com/Punpuf/hsk-syllabus-vocabulary-parser, MIT licensed, definitions from CC-CEDICT under CC BY-SA, vendored at `data/hsk_word_list.tsv` with provenance in `data/ATTRIBUTION.md` and no in-app about screen. Cited: `data/ATTRIBUTION.md` and `data/hsk_word_list.tsv` at commit 5dc8182.

D2. Only levels 1 through 6 are built into the database; the 5,622 rows the source labels `7-9` are filtered out and do not ship. Cited: `docs/measurements.md`, which records the 7-9 bucket as one undifferentiated group; filtering keeps the `level` column a plain integer 1-6 with no sentinel, so every query over it is unambiguous.

D3. After the D15 collapse, the words per level are exactly 300, 200, 500, 1000, 1600 and 1800, cumulative 300, 500, 1000, 2000, 3600 and 5400, an exact match to the published HSK 3.0 cumulative standard with no rounding. Cited: `docs/measurements.md` tables "Rows, words, and the collapse" and "Words per level", regenerated by `python3 docs/measure.py > docs/measurements.md` at commit feb876b; the published cumulative figures are reported by studycli.org, hackchinese.com and hanzistroke.com, the last of which independently states the HSK 3.0 level 1 list contains 300 entries.

D4. Pinyin is rendered with tone marks wherever it is read; numbered pinyin ships in the database reserved as the input format wherever pinyin is ever typed. Cited: review answer 2026-09-07, "I only want pinyin", "I do care about tones but not about testing them", and "for the pinyin writing, the numbers should be used and always for reading should not be numbers, regular accents".

D5. The tested direction is pinyin to English, not English to pinyin. Cited: Terai, Yamashita & Pasich 2021, Studies in Second Language Acquisition, "Effects of learning direction in retrieval practice on EFL vocabulary learning", which found L2-to-L1 more effective for lower-proficiency learners and L1-to-L2 more effective for higher-proficiency learners; review answer 2026-09-07, "near beginner". The reverse direction is checkpoint 4.

D6. The hanzi is stored for every word and rendered on every card front, small and grey, directly beneath the pinyin, never on the back and never tested. Cited: review answer 2026-09-07, "If you put hanzi on some of them, put it on all of them. Keep it all on the front small under the pinyin". Why it is shown at all: `docs/measurements.md` table "Pinyin collisions" records 5.3% of level 1, 10.3% of levels 1-3 and 13.1% of levels 1-6 sharing exact toned pinyin with another word, `shì` alone covering 是 事 市 室 试, so a pinyin-only front cannot be graded honestly on those cards.

D7. Audio is AVSpeechSynthesizer given the **hanzi** string, never the pinyin string, with the voice returned by `AVSpeechSynthesisVoice(language: "zh-CN")` at the default rate, auto-playing once on flip, with a speaker glyph on the back that replays the utterance on tap. Cited: `docs/measurements.md` table "Speech synthesis", where the same speech stack rendered 学习汉语很有意思 in 1.85s and its pinyin transliteration in 6.48s, the latter being the letters spelled out.

D8. Display definitions are derived at database build time by a rule that strips CL:/variant/see-also/abbr. clauses, parentheticals containing CJK or `[pinyin]` brackets, keeps the first two `/`-separated senses, and truncates by length, with `clean()` in `docs/measure.py` as the reference implementation. Cited: `docs/measurements.md` table "Definition cleanup rule", measured over all 5,400 shipped words, taking the median from 39 to 22 characters, p90 from 109 to 45, max from 545 to 90, with **zero** rows cleaning to empty.

D9. A trailing digit on a word in the `word` column is a source-side disambiguation suffix and is stripped at build time from both the displayed hanzi and the string handed to the synthesizer. Cited: `docs/measurements.md`, 61 such words among the 5,400 shipped, e.g. 打1, 点1, 点2, 喂1; a zh-CN voice otherwise pronounces the digit.

D10. All logic lives in a pure Swift package `LaoshuKit` that builds and tests with `swift test` and imports no UIKit or SwiftUI; the iPhone app target is a thin SwiftUI shell over it. Cited: checked 2026-09-07, `xcrun simctl list devices available` lists no iPhone runtime, so no automated check may depend on a simulator; `swift test` plus the compile gate of D11 is what a machine can actually run.

D11. The app target's automated gate is `DEVELOPER_DIR=/Applications/Xcode.app/Contents/Developer xcodebuild -scheme Laoshu -sdk iphonesimulator -destination 'generic/platform=iOS Simulator' build CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=NO`, setting `DEVELOPER_DIR` explicitly rather than relying on `xcode-select`. Cited: checked 2026-09-07, `xcodebuild -version` fails with "requires Xcode, but active developer directory is a command line tools instance" while `/Applications/Xcode.app` exists; critique round 2 finding F2.

D12. The Xcode project for the app target is created and committed by the first task, so every later task has something to compile against. Cited: the repository at 5dc8182 holds no Xcode project.

D13. The catalogue ships as a prebuilt read-only SQLite file at `data/laoshu.sqlite`, generated from the vendored TSV by `laoshu-build-db` and committed, written atomically to a temporary file and swapped in so a rerun replaces rather than appends. Cited: D8 and D9 require a transformation whose result should be inspectable; critique round 2 finding F5.

D14. Rebuild determinism is checked on content, not file bytes: `laoshu-build-db --checksum` prints a SHA-256 over the logical rows in `word_index` order, and re-running the build must print the same value. Cited: critique round 2 finding F5.

D15. The build script keeps one row per `word_index`, the row at that word's lowest level, discarding the rest, and fails if `word_index` is still not unique after collapsing; `word_index` is the word table's primary key, carried through unchanged from the source, never a SQLite rowid. Cited: `docs/measurements.md` table "Rows, words, and the collapse", which records 81 repeated `word_index` values across 83 extra rows and zero duplicate groups differing in `word`, `pinyin`, `definition_cc-cedict` or `traditional_cc-cedict`; the log exists to be replayed by checkpoint 2, which requires a stable key.

D16. A swipe writes one row to an append-only `review` table carrying `word_index`, a timestamp, and a grade, where a left swipe is `again` and a right swipe is `good`. Cited: FSRS takes a card plus a grade of again / hard / good / easy, per open-spaced-repetition/swift-fsrs `next(card:now:grade:)`; binary again/good is a supported simplification per the fsrs4anki docs, so this log is directly replayable in checkpoint 2.

D17. New words are drawn in `word_index` ascending within the selected level, each level owning a contiguous `word_index` block, and within a block the order is pinyin dictionary order, syllable first and then tone, not plain alphabetical order. Cited: `docs/measurements.md` table "`word_index` blocks"; rows 1, 2, 3 being 爱 ài, 八 bā, 爸爸 bàba.

D18. A word counts as introduced once any `review` row exists for its `word_index`; a session draws the eight lowest-`word_index` words in the chosen level that have no review row, with no separate progress table. Cited: D15 makes `word_index` stable and D16 makes the table append-only, so the existence of a row is a sound introduced-test; D23 puts both tables on one connection, and an index on `review(word_index)` keeps the anti-join cheap.

D19. A session is eight new words, drawn per D18 from the level just tapped, per session and per level, not per day and not shared across levels. Cited: review answer 2026-09-07 choosing 8; critique round 2 finding F6 cut the daily-budget machinery to route line 7, "A day at a time", which is built next.

D20. A session ends when each of its eight words has been swiped right once or swiped left three times; a left-swiped word requeues at least three cards later (or at the end, or immediately if it is the only card left), presented front-side with its flip reset, and parked without requeue on its third left swipe. Cited: critique round 1 finding F1; the bound exists so a word Mickey cannot recall cannot make a session unendable.

D21. A session has no card cap and no time cap; backlog is a scheduling problem for checkpoint 2 to smooth rather than a reason to truncate sessions. Cited: review answer 2026-09-07, "We shouldn't need a session cap, there should never be a backlog like that that ruins the app, it should be accounted for".

D22. Session state lives in memory only; closing or force-quitting the app abandons the session, though swipes already made stand in the log. Cited: critique round 2 finding F6 moved persistence of session position to route line 7; the swipes are already durable per D16, so nothing is lost but the position.

D23. Persistence is GRDB over one connection: the writable review database in Application Support is primary and `ATTACH`es the bundled catalogue read-only as schema `cat`, so every query including D18's introduced-test runs on that one connection. Cited: D13 requires opening a prebuilt file, which SwiftData cannot do without an import step; checkpoint 2's central query is "what is due now, ordered by due date", which is one SQL statement; measured comparisons reported by pistack.xyz and fatbobman.com put direct-SQLite frameworks ahead of Core Data ahead of SwiftData on read and write.

D24. The deployment target is iOS 26, and the build reaches the phone by sideloading from Xcode on the MacBook against a free Apple developer account, meaning the signature expires and the app must be rebuilt every seven days. Cited: review answer 2026-09-07, iOS 26.6.1 and a free account. The seven-day expiry is a standing friction for an app meant to be opened daily, and a paid account at $99 a year removes it; that is Mickey's call and does not block this checkpoint.

D25. The Mac mini has command line tools only, no Xcode, so it cannot run the D11 compile gate; until Xcode is installed there, tasks touching the app target are MacBook work and are not submitted to the factory. Cited: review answer 2026-09-07, "It only has the command line tools. I could get it if needed"; `factory-submit` option list, which offers only --assurance, --draft, --name, --pipeline, --planning, --project, --repo, --request-key and --spec-file.

D26. A swipe is ignored until the card has been flipped: on the front it neither advances the card nor writes a review row, and the flip is one-way for a single presentation, reset only by a requeue. Cited: critique round 3 finding F3; grading a card whose answer was never shown would write a row that D16 hands checkpoint 2 as evidence of recall that never happened.

D27. The end-of-session summary shows the count swiped right at first attempt out of the number of words the session introduced, lists the parked words by pinyin and meaning, and mentions no dates and no schedule. Cited: critique round 4 finding F4, which caught the phrase "needs another day" as residue of the daily model that round 2 cut.

D28. The look is Sprout's structure with Ink's bottom: a light ground, an elevated white rounded card holding the word, a slim progress bar across the top, and beneath the card the quiet "tap to flip" line and low-contrast "forgot"/"knew" hints rather than buttons, with swiping as the only way to grade. Cited: review answer 2026-09-07, "I like sprout with its flashcard looking box and the top progress bar, but I like the ink's bottom section".

D29. The palette is Sprout's: a cool near-white ground, a white card, a green accent for the progress fill and the affirmative side of the swipe, and a sans-serif face throughout rather than Ink's serif. Cited: review answer 2026-09-08, "Make the palette the sprout pallete".

## Not run here

The manual pass on the phone is Mickey's to run, after this closure task merges, since it needs a physical device and eyes on it. Steps:

1. The level list shows six rows with counts 300, 200, 500, 1000, 1600, 1800.
2. Tapping level 1 shows a pinyin front with the hanzi small and grey beneath it.
3. Tapping the card flips it to the meaning, with audio, and no hanzi on the back.
4. Swiping on the un-flipped front does nothing.
5. Swiping right after the flip advances to the next card.
6. Swiping left returns that card later in the same session.
7. The session ends after eight new words with a summary.
8. Tapping level 1 again shows words 9 through 16, not 1 through 8.

## Amendments after the simulator pass, 2026-09-07

The frozen plan is final, so these are recorded as amendments rather than edits to the decisions above. Both came from Mickey watching the app run in the iPhone 17 Pro simulator.

D7a. Amends D7. The speaker glyph sits on the **front**, under the pinyin and hanzi, not on the back. Auto-play still fires once, on the first flip to the meaning, and not on any later turn of the same presentation. Cited: review answer 2026-09-07, "I want the speaker button on the front with the pinyin, not the back". The speaker is a button inside the card's tap area, so it takes its own taps and replaying never flips the card.

D26a. Amends D26. Flipping is free in both directions and unlimited within a presentation, so a word can be looked at again as often as wanted. The swipe gate moves from "is showing the back" to "has shown the back at least once in this presentation", tracked by `Card.hasBeenRevealed`. A requeue clears it along with `isFlipped`, so a card coming back around must be recalled again before it can be graded. Cited: review answer 2026-09-07, "I want to be able to flip it as much as possible. Like if I wanted to see the word again". D26's reason survives intact: a card whose meaning was never shown still cannot write a review row, which is what protects the evidence D16 hands to checkpoint 2.

Manual step 4 above now reads: swiping a card that has never been flipped does nothing, and swiping one flipped back to its front after a look at the meaning does grade it.
