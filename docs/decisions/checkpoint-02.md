# Checkpoint 2 decisions: Come back tomorrow

One line per decision: what was decided, and the citation from the frozen plan, copied rather than paraphrased.

D1. Scheduling is batch-based. The words introduced on one day for one level form one batch, and the batch is the only unit that is ever scheduled. No per-word due date, no per-word memory state, and no spaced-repetition library. Cited: review answer 2026-09-08, "batch based", and "Stop with the due stuff. You are thinking about the process wrong. Just make 8 new words per day when you open it. You don't need to set a due date on each word."

D2. The ladder is two further looks: the next day, then seven days after that look. Three exposures in total, counting the day the words were introduced. Cited: the project statement's own words, "Recall the next day and later the week"; review answer 2026-09-08 accepting the ladder as written.

D3. A look counts as taken at the session's first swipe, and the batch's ladder position advances in the same transaction as that swipe's review row. Entering a level and leaving again without swiping anything writes nothing at all: no batch, no advance, no allowance spent. A session abandoned after its first swipe has still been looked at, and the cost is named under Failure modes. Cited: critique round 4 finding F2; `Laoshu/SessionView.swift` line 36 starts the session on view appearance, so advancing at the draw would let a single mis-tap on a level row burn the day's allowance and start a ladder on eight words the user never saw. Critique round 1 finding F1 required the moment be named at all, and checkpoint 1 D22 keeps session state in memory, so the first swipe is the earliest durable moment that means the user actually studied.

D4. The second look falls seven days after the date the first look was taken, not seven days after a date computed when the batch was created. A batch created on day 0 and first looked at on day 4 has its second look on day 11. Cited: review answer 2026-09-08, "Skip days and the batches wait for you"; anchoring to a missed scheduled date would compress the remaining ladder into a shorter gap than the one the user chose.

D4a. A batch's first look is never earlier than the calendar day after the wall-clock day it was created on: the first look date is one day after the later of the batch's own date and the calendar date of the moment it was created. Cited: critique round 2 finding F4. Without this, a session at 01:00 is dated to the previous day by D9, so its batch's next look lands on the current calendar day and the words return eight hours later the same morning, which is the checkpoint's headline promise failing in the user's own terms.

D5. A day at one level is eight new words plus every batch of that level whose look is due on or before today. At steady state that is twenty-four cards: today's eight new, yesterday's batch taking its first look, and the batch created eight days ago taking its second, its first look having been seven days earlier (critique round 4 finding F4). Cited: review answer 2026-09-08 accepting design line 2, "A day is 8 new words plus whichever batches are up. Steady state is 24 cards."

D6. Every word in a session, whether it came from a due batch or from the day's new draw, goes into one pool that is shuffled together by the injectable generator of checkpoint 1 D17a. There is no oldest-first ordering and no grouping by batch. Cited: critique round 1 finding F6; review answer 2026-09-08, "do them mixed", answering whether new words come before, after or among the due ones. The user's earlier answer of "most overdue" applied to choosing which eight of forty due words to show, a selection rule that D5 removes by showing all of them.

D7. Eight new words is a per-day allowance across the whole app, not per level and not per session. Studying level 1 today and then opening level 2 gives level 2's due batches and no new words. This amends checkpoint 1 D19 in both halves, the per-session half and the per-level half. Cited: review answer 2026-09-08 to Q2, "eight per day across the whole app", chosen over the per-level recommendation.

D7a. The day's allowance is spent when any level has a batch whose `created_on` is today, on D9's 04:00 basis. The eight-more button of D19 ignores this test; it is the only way past it. Cited: critique round 3 finding F1, which found the allowance asserted with no rule for reading it; review answer 2026-09-08 to Q2 for the scope of the test.

D8. There is no cap on the length of a session in this checkpoint, and this checkpoint does not smooth a backlog. A user who studies daily for a week and then stays away a week returns to at most seven batches, 56 words, plus the day's eight, in one session. Pacing that return is a route line of its own, built next, rather than work folded in here. Cited: checkpoint 1 D21, "no session cap and no time cap"; critique round 1 finding F4, which correctly holds that D21's promise to smooth a backlog in checkpoint 2 is not kept here; review answer 2026-09-08 to Q3, "pace it now, even though it means another checkpoint of machinery", which asks for the pacing and accepts its cost as a separate checkpoint. This checkpoint is already at the split limit, so folding it in would break the rule that cut the migration once already.

D9. A day begins at 04:00 local time, everywhere a day is computed, including the migration of D18. A session run at 01:00 belongs to the previous day. Cited: research 2026-09-08 on the Anki manual's Preferences page, where next-day-starts-at defaults to 4am rather than midnight specifically so that a late-night session is not handed two days of material at once; critique round 1 finding F3 for the migration half.

D10. Batch dates are stored as ISO local calendar dates, `YYYY-MM-DD`, never as timestamps. A batch is up when its next-look date is on or before today. Cited: review answer 2026-09-08, "You don't need to set a due date on each word"; research 2026-09-08 on Anki storing review cards at day granularity and reserving timestamps for same-day steps, which is the mechanism that makes opening the app an hour earlier than yesterday still show the words that are ready.

D11. Two new tables in the writable database, alongside the existing `review` table and on the same connection. `batch` holds `id`, `level`, `created_on`, `next_look_on` (null once retired), and `look_number` (1 for the next-day look, 2 for the week look, 2 with a null date once retired). `batch_word` holds `batch_id` and `word_index` as a composite primary key. Cited: checkpoint 1 D23, which puts the writable log and the attached read-only catalogue on one GRDB connection.

D12. The database gains a schema version and applies migrations in order. `LaoshuDatabase.open` today runs bare `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS` with nothing recording what has been applied, so there is no mechanism to extend. Cited: critique round 1 finding F5; `LaoshuKit/Sources/LaoshuKit/LaoshuDatabase.swift` lines 41 to 53.

D13. A batch and its rows are written at the session's first swipe, in the same transaction as that swipe's review row, and the batch holds every word the session drew rather than only the ones reached. Cited: checkpoint 1 D22, which keeps session state in memory only, so a session can end at any moment by the app being closed; writing the whole drawn set at the first swipe is what keeps a half-finished session's unreached words inside a batch that will return, rather than stranding them, while D3's reason keeps a session nobody swiped from writing anything.

D14. A word counts as introduced when it belongs to any batch. This amends checkpoint 1 D18, which used the existence of a `review` row. Cited: D18's test strands any word parked by three left swipes under checkpoint 1 D20, because the row exists so the word is never drawn as new again, while nothing in checkpoint 1 ever brings a parked word back; batch membership both repairs that and covers words drawn into a session that was abandoned before it reached them.

D15. The `review` table is unchanged and still records one append-only row per swipe. It is the audit trail and the source for a later stats screen; nothing in this checkpoint schedules from it. Cited: checkpoint 1 D16.

D16. Behaviour inside a session is unchanged. The requeue rule of checkpoint 1 D20 stands, the swipe gate of D26 and D26a stands, and the summary of D27 stands except where D24 and D25 amend it. A left swipe writes its row and requeues the card, and it does not alter any batch's position on the ladder. Cited: review answer 2026-09-08, "A left swipe still requeues the card inside the session, exactly as it does now, and it does not change the batch's schedule", accepted as design line 5.

D17. A batch retires after its second look and its words are never shown again by this checkpoint. Cited: review answer 2026-09-08 accepting design line 6. The consequence, that a word missed on all three looks is not re-taught, is recorded under Failure modes and is the reason checkpoint 9 exists.

D18. On first launch after the upgrade, existing `review` rows are replayed into batches: words are grouped by the pair of the local day of that word's first review row and the level it belongs to, each group becomes one batch created on that day at that level, and the ladder is wound forward by D2 and D4, with a look whose date has passed due at the next open and a batch whose second look is more than seven days behind retired. The migration runs in one transaction with its completion marker inside it and never runs twice. Cited: review answer 2026-09-08 to Q5, "put the per-day replay back in, accepting a sixth pull request", chosen over retiring everything; checkpoint 1 D16, which states the log "is directly replayable in checkpoint 2"; critique round 3 finding F3 for the grouping key being the pair rather than the day alone; Critique log, "Round 5, review 2026-09-08: ... Q5 rejected retiring pre-upgrade study, so D18 is the per-day replay again, which is the sixth pull request critique round 1 cut; the split is rebalanced by merging the schema work into the scheduler task and the two app-target screens into one." Note: the frozen document's own D18 paragraph still carries the earlier "one already-retired batch per level" wording from before round 5 — the edit meant to replace it did not take. The per-day replay above is what the Q5 answer and the round 5 critique log entry, both frozen text in the same document, agree was decided, and it is what shipped in "Replay the existing review log into batches" (pull request #8).

D19. The end-of-session summary carries a button that draws another eight new words from the same level, and the button is absent from every summary and every screen of a level with no unseen words left, not only from the finished-level screen of D22 (critique round 3 finding F3). Those words form a second batch dated the same day, independent of the first. There is no limit on how many times it may be pressed. Cited: review answer 2026-09-08, "I do also want a button to do 8 more if you want", and Q11, "End of session summary."

D20. Each row of the level list shows the number of words that level has waiting today, counting the words in every batch of that level whose look is due on or before today. It does not count the eight new words that a session would add. Cited: review answer 2026-09-08 choosing option (b), "Level list stays home, each row gains a due count."

D21. Every date the scheduler reads comes from an injectable today-provider whose default is the system clock. Cited: checkpoint 1 D10 requires the learning logic to be provable by `swift test` with no simulator, and a two-step ladder spanning eight days cannot otherwise be tested in less than eight days.

D22. Tapping a level that would compose a session of zero cards shows a screen naming which of three reasons applies, and none of them renders the normal summary, which would read "0 of 0 right the first time". The states: (a) the day's eight are done and nothing is waiting, though unseen words remain, which carries the eight-more button; (b) the level has no unseen words left but still has a batch on the ladder, which says when those words come back and carries no button; (c) the level has no unseen words and no batch at all, the finished level, which carries no button either in this checkpoint. Route line 9 puts its graded level test on this screen, which is where the user asked for it; nothing here builds that button, and state (c) ships bare (review annotation 2026-09-08 on the finished-level mockup, "This is where a button for a self review should be or a test"). Cited: critique round 2 finding F6 for dropping the button where it could only draw zero; critique round 4 finding F1, which found that a level whose last words were introduced today would otherwise be called finished on the very day it still owes two looks. Cited: critique round 1 finding F2; `LaoshuKit/Sources/LaoshuKit/SessionEngine.swift` lines 78 to 80, where an empty queue is `isFinished`, so a zero-card session renders `SessionSummaryView` today. What a finished level should offer beyond this is checkpoint 9's question.

D24. The end-of-session summary carries one line, and that line speaks only for the words the session introduced: it says when those come back. It says nothing about the due batches the session also held, whose next look the level list will show, and nothing about a batch that retired during the session. When a session introduced no new words the line is absent. Cited for the narrowing: critique round 3 finding F2, which found that a steady-state session holds words on three ladder positions at once and no single line is true of all three. This amends checkpoint 1 D27, which forbids the summary to mention any date or schedule. Cited: critique round 2 finding F1; D27's own reason, recorded in checkpoint 1 as critique round 4 finding F4, is that the phrase "needs another day" was residue of a daily model that had been cut from that checkpoint, so no schedule existed to describe. D2 puts a real ladder in place, so the reason for the prohibition is gone. Put to the user as Q6.

D25. In a session holding both new words and words from due batches, the summary's score counts every word the session held, not only the new ones, and the parked list may name words from a due batch. Cited: critique round 2 finding F2; `Session.drawnCount` in `LaoshuKit/Sources/LaoshuKit/SessionEngine.swift` line 59 is already every word handed to the session, so this is the behaviour that falls out of D5 unless something is added to prevent it, and nothing about a word from a batch makes it less worth counting.

D26. The level list recomputes its waiting counts every time it appears, not once at launch. A day that rolls over past 04:00 while the list is on screen and untouched is not detected until the list appears again. Cited: critique round 2 finding F3; `Laoshu/LevelListView.swift` line 9 holds the counts in `@State` loaded once by `.task`, and `SessionEngine` is not `@Observable`, so nothing can currently tell the list its numbers are stale after a session or a rollover.

D27. The closure task rewrites the README's line promising to see each word again "on the day you are about to forget it" to describe the ladder this checkpoint builds, and adds an amendments section to `docs/decisions/checkpoint-01.md` recording that D18, D19 and D27 of checkpoint 1 are amended here by D14, D7 and D24. Cited: critique round 3 finding F5 for the README, whose line 5 states the memory-model promise that D1 rejects; critique round 4 finding F3 for the decision record, which already carries an amendments convention at its foot and would otherwise state as decided three things the build contradicts.

D23. No new package dependency is added. The research that opened this checkpoint evaluated `open-spaced-repetition/swift-fsrs` and it is not used. Cited: D1.

## Not run here

The manual pass on the phone, over two days, is the user's to run after this closure task merges, since it needs a physical device, a real day boundary and eyes on it. Steps, as recorded in the closure task:

1. Study a level's eight new words and read the line saying when they return.
2. Reopen the same level the same day and get no new words, with a screen that says so rather than a score of zero.
3. Press eight more and get another eight.
4. See the waiting count drop without relaunching.
5. The next day, see sixteen waiting and find yesterday's words mixed among eight new ones.
6. Find a word swiped left three times yesterday present again today.
7. Confirm words studied before the upgrade come back on their own rungs.
8. Study one level to its eight, then open another, and get no new words there.
